Good morning, everyone, and welcome to Riverlight Library. I’m Maya Patel, the programme organiser for our Children’s Reading Weekend. I’ll begin with a little background and some advice on choosing an event, before taking you through six of this year’s sessions.

When we first discussed a festival, inviting well-known writers was one of our ideas. But that wasn’t why we established it. Parents kept telling us that the number of children’s titles was confusing: they wanted practical help finding books that would suit their particular child. So that became our original purpose. Meeting authors came later.

This year we’re using two additional venues, and teenage volunteers will introduce speakers and demonstrate materials, though adults still lead every session. The change that will make the biggest difference to families concerns age. We’ve replaced our broad groups with much narrower age bands, so the level should be a better fit.

Age is only a starting point. Reading confidence can mislead, since two equally able readers may respond differently to the same workshop. A favourite subject—animals or space, perhaps—can help you draw up a shortlist. For the final choice, think about how your child likes to participate: watching quietly, exchanging ideas, or making something themselves. That is usually the best guide.

Parents often ask what to choose for a child who avoids books. Short chapters can make a book taken home feel less daunting, and adults may stay at several events if a nervous child needs reassurance. But as a festival starting point, I’d choose a session that approaches a story through another art form—perhaps drawing, film, drama or sound. Reading then becomes part of a wider creative activity.

Before I describe the programme, one practical point: book for your child’s age on the festival date, not their age next school year. There’s a thirty-minute gap between events, but allow most of it when changing venues. All buildings provide step-free access, though some use a separate entrance. If you need somewhere quiet between sessions, the small courtyard behind the main library will remain open, and refreshments will be available nearby in the entrance hall.
