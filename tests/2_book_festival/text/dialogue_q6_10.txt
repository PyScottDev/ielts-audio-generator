ORGANISER: The first session is A Closer Look. Each group receives an illustrated page with some writing covered. Children examine colour, viewpoint and background details, then predict what happened and how a character feels. When the words are revealed, they discover how much the pictures had communicated—and sometimes how the illustrator had misled them.
Beyond the Words also begins with a page, but it’s only raw material. Children use voices, instruments and silence to create two soundscapes for one scene. Changing the sounds can make it comic, tense or mysterious. The final piece is heard, not displayed.
Small Beginnings is for children with ideas who dislike speaking to groups. They choose a character from a photograph and invent a tiny story. First they tell one partner, then four people, and finally—if they wish—the whole room. The gradually increasing audience is the point: children gain confidence without being pushed straight onto a stage.
Can You Prove It? uses two online reports of one event. Both contain unfamiliar terms, but this isn’t a vocabulary exercise. Children identify who produced each account, what evidence it offers and whether other sources confirm it. They learn not to trust information merely because it appears confidently on a screen.
In The Story Suitcase, each child takes an everyday item from an old case—a glove, key, ticket or broken watch. They decide who owned it, what that person wanted and why it mattered. Although the suitcase suggests faraway journeys, children aren’t comparing traditional tales; they’re creating a new character from the object in their hand.
Finally, Sixty Seconds starts with a written scene. Groups select the essential moments, plan a few shots and record a one-minute screen version. They may add music and briefly introduce their work before the films are shown. But the central challenge is translating writing into images and action within the time limit.
